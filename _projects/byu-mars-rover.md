## Overview

Every senior in my major at BYU is required to do a capstone project on a sponsored team. While most of these are smaller proof of concept projects, I decided to apply to participate in a competition team instead. I applied to the BYU mars rover team, which participates in the international University Rover Competition (URC).

The mars rover team has been, so far, an excellent opportunity to work on a cross-disciplinary engineering project in a large team of 40 people. We have electrical engineers, mechanical engineers, geologists, biologists, and computer scientists. My subteam alone has at least one person from each of these backgrounds. To be specific, I'm on the astrobiology subteam, where we have to collect soil samples from a depth of 10cm and run experiments to try to detect life.

I've been working with geologists and biologists on both a pre-existing life detection system and a new one that we are adding to the rover. We inherited a fluorescence detector built by last year's team (none of whom are on the module this year, so my small subteam now has complete ownership), and we are both trying to improve this detection and implement some form of chemical analysis. Right now I'm upgrading the detector with new high-power LEDs and lenses.

I should have more updates and photos for this page when the year ends and we compete. Until then, this sneak peak will have to be enough haha
